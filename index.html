How Earth to MIDI works

  

    
What it is

    
Earth and space data for live events. The page reads public live feeds and turns each reading into MIDI. Discrete events, like a quake or an asteroid pass, become notes. Continuous values, like wind speed or the Kp index, become controllers (CC, 0 to 127).


    
Quick start

    
    
      
  1. Create a virtual MIDI port: loopMIDI on Windows, or the IAC Driver on macOS.
    2. 
      
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge and press Connect MIDI. Choose your port (loopMIDI is picked automatically).
    3. 
      
  3. In your DAW, Chataigne or lighting software, enable that port as an input and listen on channels 1 to 7.
    4. 
      
  4. Press Send test chord to check the link. Panic clears any stuck notes.
    5. 
    

    
With no port selected the page runs in preview mode: the map, log and monitor still work.


    
Channels

    
1 Earthquakes, 2 Space station, 3 Space weather, 4 Natural events, 5 Weather, 6 Asteroids, 7 Solar flares and CMEs. Change any of them with the Note channel menu on its card. The test chord plays on the earthquake channel.


    
Earth and Space tabs

    
The tabs only change what you see. Every module keeps running and sending MIDI whichever tab is open. Use the On switch on a card to mute a module.


    
Module cards

    
    
      
  • Loops: quakes (last 24 hours), solar wind (last 2 hours), asteroids (next 7 days) and flares (last 7 days) replay as loops over the length you choose, so there is always something playing.
    • 
      
  • Note output: pick the data that drives pitch, velocity, length and pan, set the octave range, or turn notes off to use a module for controllers only.
    • 
      
  • Only play when: a filter, for example only quakes in the top 40% of magnitude.
    • 
    


    
Controller mapping

    
Every data stream can be sent as CC, pitch bend or aftertouch, on its own channel and CC number. Range % zooms in on the part of the data you care about and stretches it to 0 to 127. Invert flips it and Smooth slows the changes. Your setup saves automatically. Use Export and Import to move it between machines, and Reset to go back to the defaults.


    
The map

    
    
      
  • Click any dot, the ISS, the sun or the moon for details. Quakes, events and cities have a button to play just that item.
    • 
      
  • Use Layers to show or hide coastlines, day and night, the ISS ground track, the moon and more.
    • 
      
  • Press F or use Full screen for a clean display with a live monitor and log.
    • 
    


    
Keys

    
F full screen, ? this help, Esc closes popups and full screen.


    
Data sources

    
USGS earthquakes, wheretheiss.at, NOAA space weather, NASA EONET, Open-Meteo, NASA NeoWs and DONKI, and Natural Earth coastlines. An internet connection is needed. The only thing stored is your settings, in this browser.

    
NASA feeds use a shared demo key by default, which is rate-limited. Paste a free key from api.nasa.gov into the Asteroids card for faster updates.


    
If something is not working

    
    
      
  • No sound: check the port is selected here, enabled as an input in your software, and that a track is listening on that channel.
    • 
      
  • “Web MIDI not supported”: use Chrome or Edge on a desktop. If MIDI is blocked when opening the file directly, serve it from localhost.
    • 
      
  • A card shows an error: that feed is unreachable or rate-limited. The others keep working.
    • 
      
  • Too busy: raise the minimum magnitude, lengthen the loops, or narrow a module’s “Only play when” filter.
